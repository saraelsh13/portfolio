# Inquiry, uncertainty, and adaptive agents

I am Sara Elsharawy, a PhD student in Information at the University of Toronto. My interests connect human–information interaction and epistemology with questions about how human and artificial agents interpret evidence, allocate effort, and revise beliefs under uncertainty. I am exploring cognitive science and behavioural economics as foundations for these questions, alongside explainability, evaluation, and AI governance.

This is a developing research portfolio. It distinguishes implemented experiments, draft notes, and future directions. I am building my Python skills through small experiments, code walkthroughs, and reproducible evaluation.

## What this currently demonstrates

The project makes a research question executable through a synthetic environment, explicit baselines, held-out evaluation, reproducible outputs, and tests of experiment invariants. It also documents a failure condition and competing explanations for the outcome. This is a starting point for independent study, not a claim of original agent-learning research or advanced programming proficiency.

The main direction I want to investigate is how an agent selects useful evidence and recognizes when its available explanations are inadequate. Broader connections to open-ended learning and scientific discovery are future research questions, not established results of this project.
